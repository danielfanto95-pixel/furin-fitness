📖 How to Sell Furin Fitness

The pitch that works: Daniel lost 100+ lbs over 6 years by building a real system, not following a program — that story is the credibility. Don't sell "personal training," sell the system: nutrition, sleep, recovery, training, and mindset tracked together, with AI-augmented check-ins baked in. Who to talk to: people who've already tried a generic program or trainer and know that's not what's missing. That's your best-fit lead — not someone who's never tried anything. What NOT to do (per the partner agreement): never guarantee specific results, never make exaggerated claims, and always follow FTC disclosure rules when sharing your referral link (a simple "affiliate link" or "partner link" disclosure is enough).
Commission: 33% of the initial purchase on each qualified referral (new customer, using your link, payment processed and not refunded/disputed). Recurring/renewal purchases aren't commissionable unless separately agreed. Paid monthly within 15 business days of month close.

🎯 Who to Target

The best-fit lead is someone who has ALREADY tried a generic program, app, or trainer and knows that wasn't the missing piece. That prior-attempt signal matters more than fitness level, age, or goal type. Good signals: "I've tried everything," "I have a gym membership I don't use," "I started [program] and stopped after a few weeks," "I know what to do, I just don't follow through." Weaker fit: someone who has genuinely never tried anything before and is still in the "just deciding if I want to start" phase — they may need more nurturing before a direct pitch lands.

🛡️ Objections — What to Say

"It's too expensive"

Reframe: $299 for 12 weeks works out to about $25/week — less than most people spend on takeout or a few coffees a week. It's individualized programming plus a coach personally reviewing check-ins weekly, not just an app subscription. If they still hesitate: mention the 3-payment option ($99 x3) and the lower-commitment Community option ($49/month) as an alternative entry point. Never discount or promise a deal yourself — you don't have authority to modify pricing. Direct pricing questions to Daniel.

"I've tried programs before and they didn't work"

This is actually the ideal lead, not an objection to overcome — say so directly: "That's exactly who this is built for. Generic programs and templates are the problem, not you — this is individualized programming with someone actually checking your work weekly." Don't argue or minimize their past experience. Validate it, then position the difference (individualization + real accountability) as the reason this time is different.

"I don't have time"

"The program is built around your actual schedule, not a fixed one-size-fits-all plan. That's part of what 'individualized' means here — it accounts for how much time you realistically have." If they're still unsure, the free 15-minute consult call is a low-commitment next step — they don't have to decide about the full program right now, just have a conversation.

"Is this legit? / Are you just doing this for the commission?"

Be transparent — this builds trust, it doesn't hurt your credibility: "Yes, I do earn a commission if you sign up through my link — full disclosure. But I only partner with things I'd actually recommend to a friend, and this is one of them." Point them to Daniel's own story/credibility (130 lb personal transformation, NCCPT/ISSA/NASM certified, 10+ years coaching) and the free consult call as a no-pressure way to verify it themselves.

"I don't know if it'll work for me specifically"

Never guarantee specific results — that's a hard rule (FTC + partner agreement). Instead: "I can't promise a specific result, nobody honestly can, but the program adjusts to how you're actually responding rather than following a fixed plan regardless of what's working." The free consult call exists specifically to answer "will this work for my situation" — encourage them to ask Daniel directly rather than you speculating.

"I need to think about it"

Don't pressure or create fake urgency you don't control (spots/deadlines are Daniel's call, not yours to invent). Instead: "Totally fair — no rush. If it'd help, the free consult call is a no-pressure way to get your questions answered before deciding anything." Follow up once, politely, after a few days if you haven't heard back — don't repeatedly push.
